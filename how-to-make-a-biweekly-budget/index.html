How to Make a Biweekly Budget (and Use Your 3-Paycheck Months)

If you’re paid every two weeks, a monthly budget will feel off. Some months one paycheck has to cover rent, and some years you’ll notice a month with three paydays. A biweekly budget plans around how you’re actually paid.

How biweekly pay works

You get paid 26 times a year. Most months have two paychecks, but two months a year have three. That means if you build your budget on two paychecks a month, you get two “bonus” paychecks every year.

Step 1: Split your bills into two groups

List your fixed bills with due dates. Then split them:

  • Paycheck A pays the bills due roughly from the 1st to the 14th.
  • Paycheck B pays the bills due roughly from the 15th to the 31st.

Rent is usually the biggest problem, because it’s due on the 1st and is often more than half a paycheck. A common fix: set aside half of rent from each paycheck, so the full amount is ready by the 1st.

Step 2: Even out the two halves

If paycheck A is carrying much more than paycheck B, ask some companies to move your due date. Phone, utilities, insurance and credit cards usually will. The goal is two paychecks that each leave a similar amount for groceries, gas and savings.

Step 3: Budget the rest of each paycheck

After bills, give the rest a job: spending money, savings and extra debt payments. Plan each paycheck down to $0 left to assign. See how to budget paycheck to paycheck for the full method.

Step 4: Plan your 3-paycheck months now

Look up which months you’ll get a third paycheck this year and decide today what it’s for. Good choices:

  • Finish your $500 emergency fund.
  • Make a big payment on your highest-priority debt.
  • Top up a sinking fund, like Christmas or car repairs.

If you don’t decide in advance, the extra paycheck usually just disappears.

Common mistakes

  • Treating every month the same. Your income isn’t the same every month, so your plan shouldn’t be.
  • Forgetting the timing of big bills. A bill due on the 2nd needs to come out of the paycheck before it.
  • Spending the third paycheck by accident. Give it a job before it arrives.
