What Are Sinking Funds? 12 Every Family Should Have

Most “emergencies” aren’t really surprises. Christmas is on December 25 every year. Cars need tires. Car insurance renews. Kids have birthdays.

A sinking fund is money you set aside a little at a time for a cost you know is coming. When the bill arrives, the money is already there, so it never goes on a credit card.

How to calculate a sinking fund

It’s one simple division:

Amount needed ÷ months until you need it = save per month

If you want $600 for Christmas and it’s 10 months away, that’s $60 a month, or about $30 a paycheck if you’re paid every two weeks.

12 sinking funds to consider

  1. Emergency fund. Not technically a sinking fund, but start here. Aim for $500 to $1,000 first.
  2. Christmas and holidays. Gifts, travel, food.
  3. Car repairs and maintenance. Tires, brakes, oil changes. $50 to $100 a month is common.
  4. Car insurance and registration. Paying yearly is often cheaper than monthly.
  5. Medical and dental. Deductibles, copays, glasses.
  6. Birthdays and gifts. Kids’ parties, weddings, baby showers add up fast.
  7. Back to school. Clothes, supplies, fees.
  8. Home repairs. Appliances break. Renters can use this for moving costs.
  9. Pets. Vet visits, food, boarding.
  10. Vacation. Pay for the trip before you go and enjoy it more.
  11. New phone or laptop. Replace it before it dies.
  12. Annual subscriptions. Amazon Prime, memberships, software renewals.

You don’t need all 12 at once

Pick the three that have hurt you most in the past year. If last December went on a credit card, start with Christmas. If a car repair wiped you out, start there.

Where to keep the money

A free high-yield savings account works well. Many online banks let you create separate “buckets” inside one account, so each fund has its own name and balance. If not, one savings account plus a tracker to know how much belongs to each fund is enough.

Does your plan fit your budget?

Add up the monthly amount for every fund. If the total is more than you can save, either push a date later or lower a target. It’s better to fully fund three goals than to half-fund eight.
