Debt Snowball vs. Avalanche: Which Pays Off Debt Faster? (Real Example)

We ran the numbers on $18,050 of real-world debt. Here's how the snowball and avalanche methods compare on time and total interest, and how to choose.

There are two popular ways to pay off several debts at once. Both work. The difference is the order you pay them in.

  • Debt snowball: pay off the smallest balance first.
  • Debt avalanche: pay off the highest interest rate first.

In both, you pay the minimum on everything and throw every extra dollar at one target debt. When it’s gone, its payment “rolls over” to the next one, so your payoff speeds up over time.

A real example

Here are five typical debts, $18,050 in total:

Debt Balance APR Minimum
Credit card $4,200 24.9% $110
Car loan $8,900 7.5% $245
Medical bill $650 0% $50
Store card $1,300 29.99% $40
Personal loan $3,000 12% $95

The minimums add up to $540 a month. Say you can find $200 extra each month, for $740 total.

Snowball order: medical bill → store card → personal loan → credit card → car loan. Avalanche order: store card → credit card → personal loan → car loan → medical bill.

The results

Snowball Avalanche
Months to debt-free 30 29
Total interest paid $3,219 $2,790

The avalanche finishes one month sooner and saves about $429 in interest. That’s real money, but it isn’t huge.

These figures come from a month-by-month calculation. Your lender may calculate interest slightly differently, so treat them as close estimates.

So which should you choose?

Choose the avalanche if you’re motivated by math and your high-interest debts aren’t huge. You’ll pay the least interest.

Choose the snowball if you need quick wins to keep going. In the example above, the medical bill is gone in under a year with the snowball. Seeing a debt disappear early is what keeps a lot of people from quitting, and a plan you stick with beats a “perfect” plan you abandon.

The bigger lever is the extra payment. In most cases, adding another $50 to $100 a month changes your debt-free date far more than switching methods does.

How to start this week

  1. List every debt with its balance, interest rate and minimum.
  2. Find your extra amount, even $25. Look at your paycheck budget for leaks.
  3. Pick a method and write the order down.
  4. Set up autopay for every minimum so you never miss one.
  5. Put your extra on debt #1 the day you get paid.
